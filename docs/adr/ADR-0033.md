# ADR-0033: Configurable Outlier-Rejected Delta Smoothing and Fixed-Accumulator Interpolation ($\alpha$)

> Raw frame-to-frame deltas suffer from micro-jitter caused by OS background threads and driver
> buffer queues, while physics simulations explode if fed variable deltas or unbounded sub-steps
> during a CPU stall ("Spiral of Death"). The clock subsystem provides selectable delta
> smoothing (Raw, Exponential Moving Average, or Unreal-style Outlier-Rejected Rolling Window)
> alongside a deterministic Fixed-Timestep Accumulator that outputs discrete step counts capped by
> `maxSubSteps` and a normalized interpolation factor $\alpha \in [0.0, 1.0)$ for smooth visual
> rendering between fixed physics ticks.

![Proposed][badge-proposed]

## Context

Real-time interactive engines must reconcile two conflicting timing models: variable hardware
display refresh cadences and deterministic fixed-rate simulation systems. Sampling raw operating
system clocks produces erratic intervals across consecutive frames. At the same time, numerical
physics integrators require uniform time deltas to prevent numerical divergence.

### The Micro-Jitter Problem

Even when running on displays locked to 60 Hz or 144 Hz with vertical synchronization (VSync)
enabled, the raw elapsed time between consecutive frames oscillates. A display operating at
60 Hz nominally presents frames every 16.666 milliseconds. In practice, monotonic OS clock
samples capture values such as 14.2 ms, 18.9 ms, 15.1 ms, and 17.5 ms.

This variance stems from several hardware and operating system factors:

- **Operating system scheduler interrupts:** Background processes, Deferred Procedure Calls
  (DPCs), and thread context switches interrupt the engine thread between timer queries.
- **GPU presentation queue latency:** Swapchain buffer flips via DXGI, Vulkan, or Metal do
  not complete at instantaneous intervals. Presentation queues introduce variable wait times
  depending on desktop compositors (such as Windows DWM) and driver queue depth.
- **Clock sampling granularity:** Although high-resolution platform counters report nanosecond
  values, the instruction cost of querying the counter and returning to user mode varies by
  several CPU cycles.

Feeding raw, oscillating delta times directly into camera transforms, character controllers,
and procedural animation produces visible micro-stutter. The human visual cortex detects
sub-millisecond cadence irregularities as jerky movement, even when average throughput exceeds
120 frames per second.

### The Fixed Timestep and Spiral of Death

Rigid-body physics engines (such as PhysX, Jolt, and Box2D) solve constraint systems using
numerical integration (e.g., Symplectic Euler). These integrators assume a constant delta time
($\Delta t$). If fed a variable delta time, the simulation exhibits severe artifacts:

- **Constraint solver instability:** Varying $\Delta t$ breaks spring-damper equations and joint
  constraints, injecting false kinetic energy into the simulation and causing objects to jitter
  or explode.
- **Non-deterministic tunneling:** Fast-moving colliders penetrate geometry when a long frame
  delta expands their displacement beyond swept volume collision limits.

To solve this, game engines decouple physics from rendering using a fixed-timestep accumulator.
The engine measures elapsed wall-clock time, adds it to an internal accumulator, and executes
physics steps in discrete increments of a fixed duration ($dt_{\text{fixed}}$, such as 16.666 ms
for 60 Hz) while the accumulator holds sufficient time:

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->
```cpp
m_accumulator += frameDelta;
while (m_accumulator >= m_fixedStepDuration)
{
    stepPhysics(m_fixedStepDuration);
    m_accumulator -= m_fixedStepDuration;
}
```
<!-- markdownlint-enable MD013 -->

This naive loop creates a failure state known as the **Spiral of Death**:

1. A heavy operational spike occurs, such as a blocking disk read, background asset streaming
   stall, or shader compilation hitch, lasting 100 ms.
2. On the next frame, the accumulator holds 100 ms. The loop attempts 6 consecutive physics
   steps ($6 \times 16.666\text{ ms} = 100\text{ ms}$).
3. Computing 6 physics steps requires significant CPU time (e.g., 50 ms), causing the entire
   frame to take 150 ms.
4. On the subsequent frame, the accumulator receives 150 ms, requiring 9 physics steps.
5. Physics execution time increases further, forcing the loop to request even more steps on the
   next frame until the engine freezes completely.

The engine requires a timekeeping architecture that eliminates micro-jitter from render deltas,
guards the accumulator against the Spiral of Death, and provides smooth visual transforms
between fixed simulation steps.

## Decision

We introduce selectable delta smoothing algorithms within `EngineClock` and a bounded
fixed-timestep accumulator with render interpolation ($\alpha$) within `SimulationClock`.

### Selectable Delta Smoothing Modes

`EngineClock` provides four delta smoothing strategies via `DeltaSmoothingMode`:

1. **`None`:** Uses the exact, unfiltered hardware delta time, clamped only by `maxFrameDelta`
   (default 250 ms, protecting against debugger breakpoints). Useful for raw performance
   profiling and diagnostic benchmarks.
2. **`ExponentialMovingAverage`:** Applies a single-pole low-pass filter blending 90% of the
   previous smoothed delta with 10% of the current clamped delta:
   $$\Delta t_{\text{smoothed}} = 0.90 \cdot \Delta t_{k-1} + 0.10 \cdot \Delta t_{\text{clamped}}$$
   This dampens high-frequency jitter while following gradual frame rate drift.
3. **`OutlierRejectedAverage` (Default):** Adopts the rolling window filter proven in Unreal
   Engine 5 (`kDeltaHistorySize = 11u`). The clock stores the 11 most recent frame deltas in a
   fixed circular buffer. When computing the smoothed delta, the algorithm identifies and discards
   the minimum and maximum outliers, then averages the remaining 9 samples:
   $$
   \Delta t_{\text{smoothed}} =
   \frac{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \Delta t_i - \min(\Delta t) - \max(\Delta t)}{N - 2}
   $$
   This eliminates single-frame hitch pollution. A 200 ms garbage collection or shader hitch is
   discarded as the maximum outlier, preventing it from artificially inflating delta times over
   subsequent frames.
4. **`FixedDeterministic`:** Ignores hardware timer variance entirely, advancing by an exact
   rational interval ($1 / \text{targetFps}$) on every tick. This mode is required for headless
   unit tests, automated regression runs, offline video capture, and lockstep multiplayer
   replays.

### Deterministic Fixed-Step Accumulator with Spiral-of-Death Guard

`SimulationClock` implements fixed-timestep accumulation via `consumeFixedSteps()`. Subsystems
configure the fixed rate and execution ceiling through `configureFixedStep(rate, maxSubSteps)`:

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->
```cpp
FixedStepResult SimulationClock::consumeFixedSteps() noexcept
{
    FixedStepResult result{};
    result.fixedDelta = m_fixedStepDuration;

    if (m_fixedStepDuration.nanoseconds <= 0) [[unlikely]]
    {
        return result;
    }

    // Drain discrete fixed steps up to the configured limit
    while (m_accumulator >= m_fixedStepDuration && result.stepCount < m_maxSubSteps)
    {
        m_accumulator -= m_fixedStepDuration;
        ++result.stepCount;
    }

    // Spiral-of-Death guard: clamp excess accumulated time if ceiling was reached
    if (m_accumulator >= m_fixedStepDuration) [[unlikely]]
    {
        m_accumulator = Duration(m_accumulator.nanoseconds % m_fixedStepDuration.nanoseconds);
        result.wasClamped = true;
    }

    // Compute normalized render interpolation alpha
    result.interpolationAlpha = static_cast<gp::Float32>(
        static_cast<gp::Float64>(m_accumulator.nanoseconds) /
        static_cast<gp::Float64>(m_fixedStepDuration.nanoseconds)
    );

    return result;
}
```
<!-- markdownlint-enable MD013 -->

If a frame hitch forces the accumulator past `maxSubSteps * fixedDelta`, the guard drains the
maximum allowed steps, discards the excess time using integer modulo, and flags `wasClamped = true`
in `FixedStepResult`. The physics simulation falls behind real time during the hitch to prevent
an escalating execution cascade.

### Normalized Render Interpolation Factor ($\alpha$)

Because display refresh rates rarely match the physics simulation frequency, the accumulator
retains a fractional remainder after draining all discrete steps:

$$
\alpha =
\frac{\text{m\_accumulator.nanoseconds}}{\text{m\_fixedStepDuration.nanoseconds}} \in [0.0, 1.0)
$$

Graphics and presentation systems use $\alpha$ to linearly blend transform states between the
previous physics state ($P_{\text{previous}}$) and current physics state ($P_{\text{current}}$):

$$P_{\text{render}} = (1 - \alpha) P_{\text{previous}} + \alpha P_{\text{current}}$$

Dynamic entities retain transform snapshots from the previous and current physics ticks.
Interpolation introduces up to one fixed step of visual latency ($16.666\text{ ms}$ at 60 Hz),
which prevents visual jitter and avoids extrapolation overshoots. This guarantees smooth motion
on high refresh displays (120 Hz, 144 Hz, 240 Hz) while physics ticks at an invariant 60 Hz.

### Architectural Invariants

1. **Separation of Hardware Sampling and Domain Advancement:** Hardware monotonic timers are
   queried once per frame by `EngineClock::tick()`. Raw deltas are clamped to `maxFrameDelta`
   and filtered through the active `DeltaSmoothingMode` before advancing domain clocks. Raw
   unfiltered deltas are never exposed to gameplay or physics domains.
2. **Bounded Sub-Step Execution (Spiral-of-Death Prevention):** The fixed accumulator executes
   at most `maxSubSteps` (default 8) iterations in a single frame. When accumulated time exceeds
   this threshold, the clock clamps remaining duration via integer modulo arithmetic and sets
   `wasClamped = true`.
3. **Exact Normalized Render Interpolation Factor ($\alpha$):** The fractional remainder
   remaining in the accumulator is normalized against `m_fixedStepDuration` to produce
   $\alpha \in [0.0, 1.0)$. Render systems read $\alpha$ to interpolate between past and current
   physics transforms without modifying physics state.
4. **Isolated Deterministic Time Progression:** When configured with `FixedDeterministic`, the
   clock advances by constant rational durations regardless of hardware time elapsed. Coupled
   with fixed-step physics, this ensures identical simulation behavior across repeated runs.

### Comparative Smoothing Mode Matrix

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->
| Smoothing Mode | Resistance to Hitches | Latency to Frame-Rate Shift | Physics Stability | Suitability for Replays |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`None` (Raw Delta)** | None (passes full spikes) | Zero (immediate) | Poor (causes sub-step bursts) | Unsuitable (clock variance) |
| **`ExponentialMovingAverage`** | Moderate (exponential decay) | High (asymptotic lag) | Moderate (smooths drift) | Unsuitable (float blend) |
| **`OutlierRejectedAverage`** | High (drops min and max) | Low (adapts in 3-5 frames) | High (stable step count) | Unsuitable (hardware noise) |
| **`FixedDeterministic`** | Absolute (ignores hardware) | Constant (zero latency) | Deterministic (exact interval) | Optimal (bit-identical) |

## Alternatives Considered

### Option A: Variable Delta Physics (`updatePhysics(dt)`)

Pass variable frame delta time directly to the physics solver on each frame.

**Why rejected:** Variable time steps destabilize constraint solvers, inject synthetic energy into
rigid-body springs, and cause high-velocity objects to tunnel through thin geometry during frame
drops. It also destroys bitwise reproducibility across runs.

### Option B: Unbounded Fixed Accumulator (`while (acc >= dt) stepPhysics()`)

Implement a fixed-timestep accumulator without a maximum sub-step ceiling or clamping mechanism.

**Why rejected:** Triggers the Spiral of Death when a long frame (e.g., asset loading hitch or
shader compilation) causes physics sub-steps to exceed the available frame budget. Physics
workload compounds on subsequent frames until the engine locks up.

### Option C: Transform Extrapolation

Predict future object transforms ahead of the latest physics state using current velocities:
$P_{\text{render}} = P_{\text{current}} + V_{\text{current}} \cdot \alpha \cdot dt_{\text{fixed}}$.

**Why rejected:** Extrapolation overshoots when an entity experiences abrupt acceleration,
collision impacts, or sudden stops within the sub-step interval. When the physics engine
subsequently resolves the collision, extrapolation causes visible snapping and position popping.
Interpolation between two verified physics states introduces a fraction of a frame of visual
delay but remains strictly bounded and visually continuous.

## Consequences

### Positive

- **Smooth visual presentation across arbitrary refresh rates.** Outlier-rejected smoothing
  removes OS timer jitter while render interpolation ($\alpha$) produces continuous movement
  on 120 Hz, 144 Hz, and 240 Hz displays without increasing physics tick rates.
- **Stable and deterministic physics integration.** Rigid-body constraint solvers always advance
  by an exact rational duration ($dt_{\text{fixed}}$), preserving conservation of energy and
  preventing collision tunneling.
- **Protected execution budget under severe hitches.** The `maxSubSteps` ceiling and modulo clamp
  prevent the engine from entering an escalating Spiral of Death during asset streaming delays
  or debugger pauses.
- **Hitch isolation without lag accumulation.** Discarding the minimum and maximum outliers
  in the 11-frame window prevents single-frame stalls from polluting subsequent delta calculations.
- **Reproducible headless testing.** The `FixedDeterministic` mode advances time in constant
  rational increments, enabling identical simulation outputs across platforms for regression
  tests and lockstep multiplayer replays.

### Negative

- **Single-frame visual display latency.** Interpolation blends between $P_{\text{previous}}$ and
  $P_{\text{current}}$, which delays rendered geometry by up to one physics tick
  (16.666 ms at 60 Hz) relative to the most recent simulation state.
- **Memory overhead for transform buffering.** Dynamic entities must store both previous and
  current transform states ($P_{\text{previous}}$ and $P_{\text{current}}$) to support render
  interpolation, increasing per-body memory usage.
- **Simulation time loss during heavy hitches.** When the Spiral-of-Death guard triggers
  (`wasClamped = true`), unsimulated time is dropped, causing physics simulation time to lag
  wall-clock time until the system recovers.
